# Why preserve both C and B score distributions?

Date: 2026-10-27. Decision record for
[SPEC-0005](../../specs/0005-relative-score-distributions.md), implemented on 2026-10-27.

The immediate goal is to compare which functional clusters a query matches,
using the signatures already being computed. Raw composite scores are specific
to each cluster: different dimensions, means, spreads, and signature sizes
produce different score distributions. Sorting clusters by raw compatibility
alone does not establish comparable membership evidence.

## What the two populations contribute

A background-relative upper-tail fraction answers: “What fraction of this
cluster's sampled nonmembers match at least as strongly as the query?” A small
fraction indicates an unusual background match. This directly uses B scores;
C affects the result only indirectly through signature fitting and selection.

A cluster-relative percentile answers: “What fraction of known members score
at or below the query?” A low percentile warns that the query scores below most
known members, even if the B-tail evidence looks strong. A high percentile means
strong member compatibility; it is not a membership probability. These two
quantities should be reported separately, rather than multiplied into an
unsupported combined probability.

For illustration, hypothetical matches may look like this:

| Cluster | Raw score | B upper-tail fraction | C percentile | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A | 0.70 | 0.10 | 0.60 | Typical of members, but also common in B |
| B | 0.35 | 0.0001 | 0.55 | Strong B-relative evidence and member compatibility |
| C | 0.20 | 0.0001 | 0.01 | Strong B-relative evidence, but unusually low for members |

These are invented examples, not experimental results. They show why a lower raw
score can be more informative and why B evidence alone leaves useful information
out. No automatic rule choosing B over C is established by the example.

## Why this can be calculated after discovery

Once the final signature's dimensions, means, widths, and weights are fixed,
scoring C and B is deterministic. Existing embeddings and exact membership
lists are sufficient. No PLM inference, dimension ranking, backward removal,
threshold fitting, or fold rotation is needed to reconstruct these distributions.

Before this extension, the implementation computed background scores to evaluate
signatures, but persisted aggregate metrics and thresholds rather than the complete
composite B-score distribution. It does preserve final parameters, per-dimension background
statistics, setup-data pools/embeddings, and fold OOF-positive scores. Therefore
the missing final C/B distributions can be backfilled without discarding the
existing discovery effort.

The extension is now implemented in `build-library` after the final signature is
fixed. It persists sorted float64 log scores, preserving ties and window multiplicity.
Log space prevents ordinary Gaussian-score underflow from destroying useful
ordering. Roughly 5.5 GiB stores all final B arrays for this reference collection;
C adds comparatively little. These are useful retained outputs and must survive
the recently introduced disposable Gaussian/prefix cache cleanup.

## Role of the existing five rotations

The five rotations provide stability and internal held-out-positive diagnostics.
They are not prerequisites for either final C/B distribution. The earlier plan
was to keep them mandatory and run them once. That plan was superseded by the
user-approved revision of [SPEC-0004](../../specs/0004-build-library.md): one all-C
fit is now the implementation target for default production, and five-fold work
is optional research, usually for selected clusters. Compatible completed final
fits are reused rather than discarded. The revised final-only default is now implemented; five-fold work runs only
when requested. The all-C signature remains the
query signature in both modes.

Do not merge the five OOF-positive distributions into the final C distribution:
they were generated by different fitted signatures. They remain useful separate
validation records, not automatically a calibration reference for final scores.

## Limits and practical decision

Final C scores are in-sample. B was also used during feature selection, so a
small empirical B-tail fraction can overstate evidence on new nonmembers. The
plus-one tail convention avoids zeros, but does not make it a valid independent
p-value. Cluster-capped B is a chosen reference mixture, not deployment prevalence.

Actual membership probabilities would require an evaluated calibration approach,
appropriate positive/negative calibration data and assumptions about query
frequencies. Standard probability calibration maps classifier scores using data
not used to fit the underlying classifier; reusing fitting data biases that
mapping. See the [scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

For immediate use, preserve both full empirical distributions and expose the
explicit quantities in SPEC-0005. This supports background-evidence comparison
with member compatibility alongside it, including ambiguous and tied matches.
It does not require introducing a probability model, forced single-label output,
a new background sample, or another expensive signature-discovery run.
